# Six enquiries this month and no idea where they came from

Six enquiries. Or eleven. Or maybe you're not even sure it's six.

Ask yourself honestly: last quarter, can you name the single enquiry that turned

into your best customer? Can you say which piece of marketing it came from?

If the answer is "I think it was Facebook, but actually someone mentioned the

referral" — then you don't have a marketing problem or a marketing success. You

have a recording problem, and it is quietly making every other decision

worse.

Why this is worse than it looks

Without per-enquiry provenance, three expensive beliefs survive that should die:

"The website is the problem." Maybe. You can't tell, because the one enquiry

that became a 10k job came from a Facebook comment.

"We should do SEO." Maybe. But you don't know whether the two leads search

produced were the two that mattered or the two that didn't.

"The newsletter doesn't work." It produced one lead six months ago, possibly.

Each of these beliefs is unfalsifiable, and each one is expensive. An unfalsifiable

belief is a standing instruction to keep spending against it. **You will keep

funding the channel that produces volume and abandon the one that produces

revenue, because volume is the only thing you can actually see.**

The fix is smaller than it sounds

Every enquiry needs five fields. Not a CRM. Five fields:

  • timestamp — exact, not "last Tuesday"
  • source — which channel, one value from a fixed list
  • what they asked for
  • what happened — quoted / booked / no reply / hired / lost
  • outcome — if hired, roughly what it was worth

That's it. One line per enquiry, in any spreadsheet that will actually get

opened.

Ninety days later you will have the thing most businesses in your position are

paying an agency to tell them, except you'll know it about your business and

it will have cost you nothing.

record forward, not recovering it. The absence of history is not a reason not to

begin — it's the reason that most of your competitors never will.
