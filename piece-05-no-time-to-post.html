# You don't post because posting takes time you don't have

You've meant to start posting. You set up the account. It's been four months.

The reason isn't discipline and it isn't laziness. **The reason is that posting

is a second unpaid job with no deadline, and your actual job has a deadline at

4pm.** You will always do the thing with the deadline. That's not a character

flaw, it's arithmetic.

So let's drop the goal of posting more and deal with what's actually stuck.

Why "post 3x a week" has failed you four times

You've picked a cadence you can't sustain at the worst possible moment — when

you're busiest. And so the failure is predictable: a good week, two, then a

crash.

Most people respond to this by picking a lower cadence, usually one that still

sounds respectable when an imaginary client asks. Still doomed. The constraint

was never frequency. **The constraint is that the whole job sits in your head

until 9pm.**

What would actually work

Two posts a month, both written on the same Sunday, in one hour, from three

questions you answer anyway:

  • What did a customer ask this month that surprised you?
  • What did you get wrong on a job?
  • What would you tell someone who called you about this last month?

Those answers are already true and already said out loud to actual customers.

A post is just the same sentence with the customer-name removed.

The alternative worth considering

You may be better off not posting at all, and instead fixing the one thing a

stranger encounters before they ever see a post: **what happens when they try

to reach you.**

A business that replies instantly to a 9pm email beats a business with a lovely

feed. The first one is closer to a sale in every measurable way, and it doesn't

cost you an hour a month.

Fix the reply. Post when you have something. Neither requires you to become

somebody else on the internet.
