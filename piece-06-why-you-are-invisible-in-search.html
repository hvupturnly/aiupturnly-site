# Why you're invisible in search (it isn't your keywords)

You've looked at your website and concluded the problem is keywords.

It's almost certainly not. Here's the actual mechanic of why a 5–50 person

service business doesn't show up when a customer searches, and it has almost

nothing to do with words.

Search ranks answers, not businesses

When someone searches "emergency plumber near me," Google is not deciding which

plumber is best. It has already decided the shape of the answer, and your

job is to match that shape or not appear.

Most small service sites don't match the shape. They have a homepage that says

"Welcome to [Company], providing quality services since 2009." Search has no

place to put that. There is no question it answers.

A page titled "Emergency plumbing in [city] — what it costs and how fast"

answers a question. That page can rank. The homepage cannot.

The three questions your customers actually ask

Every one of your enquiries came from someone with a question, not someone

browsing. Almost certainly they asked:

1. How much is this? ("Roughly what does a boiler replacement cost?")

2. How soon can you come? ("Emergency, tonight, bank holiday.")

3. Are you any good / will you turn up? — which is a trust question, and

the hardest one for a stranger to get answered.

Answer those three, in those words, on pages that exist. That is the whole

of it. Not a keyword list. Not a plugin. Not a rebuild.

Why owners rebuild the website instead

Because a rebuild feels like progress and it is much harder to measure. Fixing

an enquiry path produces no visible artefact. There is nothing to show a

partner, and nothing to photograph.

So the owner spends a Saturday on a new site, feels like the business is moving

forward, and the enquiries arrive at the same speed they always did.

The uncomfortable order of operations

The pages are second. If you are invisible in search because your response time

is bad, then being findable just sends people to a slow door. **Traffic to a

broken enquiry path is worse than no traffic** — it produces losses you'd never

have had.

Fix what happens when they arrive. Then build the pages.
