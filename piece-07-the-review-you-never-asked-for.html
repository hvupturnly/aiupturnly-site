# Your best customer is one who never wrote a review

Somewhere in your inbox there are people who think you're excellent.

They used you. They were happy. They told a friend, most likely, because

telling a friend is free and easy and writing a review is neither. Then the

moment passed, and you never heard from them again.

**That is the cheapest, highest-conversion marketing you have, and you are

letting it expire.**

Why they never wrote one

Almost nobody writes a review unprompted, and it is not disloyalty. Writing a

review is a task with no reward and mild social risk — you're asking the client

to spend effort on your behalf in public. Even delighted clients skip it.

So the absence of reviews tells you nothing about your quality. It tells you

about your asking.

The ask that actually works

Not "leave us a review." That is a request to do work, and work gets declined.

Ask at the moment of peak enthusiasm, and make it a one-word job:

> "Glad it worked out. If you have thirty seconds, would you put two lines

> about it on Google? It genuinely helps us more than anything else we do."

Three things make the difference: timing (right after the good news),

size (two lines, not an essay), and specificity (name the platform and

tell them exactly where).

Send it once, in the same message as your invoice or your confirmation, and

then do not chase. One follow-up at most. Chasing for a review you did not earn

on the job is how you get a review that damages you.

The one to collect right now

Go back through last year's jobs. Pick the five you were most proud of. Message

each one.

Most of them will be delighted. Some will be surprised. That reaction — "I

thought you'd ask the difficult customers" — is worth knowing, because it

tells you exactly how your service lands.
