# Your competitor answered in two minutes

Not better. Not cheaper. Earlier.

You got the enquiry at 4:12pm. They got it at 4:12pm. By 4:14 they had sent

something — you still don't know what, and you never will — and by Thursday

they had the job.

The uncomfortable thing about this story is that you will spend the next three

years thinking about price or quality or your website, and none of those were

the variable.

What the two minutes actually contained

Probably almost nothing. A receipt, not a proposal.

> "Got your message — I'm on a job until 5, then I'll call you back properly

> and we'll work out the times."

That's the whole thing. It doesn't win the job on merit. It wins it on not

losing it while you were driving.

Why this keeps happening to good businesses

Because good businesses are staffed by people who are doing the work, and

the work is what makes them good. The enquiry sits in a pocket until the pocket

has a free hand.

The response time of a business is therefore a direct measure of how absorbed

its owner is. Which means: **the more successful you become, the slower you get,

unless you deliberately separate the two.**

The three-minute fix that is not a new hire

Put one message on your phone that goes out without you. You approve it once,

you never think about it again, and every enquiry gets a receipt inside the

window it needs to survive.

This is not automation in the way that word usually means. Nobody is writing

anything persuasive. It is a receipt, sent instantly, by a machine that has no

better thing to do at 4:14pm.

You keep every enquiry. You keep talking to them as a person at 5. **The quality

stays exactly as good. The speed stops depending on where you are.**
