# The one page that earns its keep

Every small business I look at has the same problem in a different costume. Not

too little marketing. **Too much marketing, spread over too many places, none

of which anybody remembers.**

But you don't need a content strategy. You need one page that works.

What one page has to do

Three things. That's the whole specification.

1. Say what you do, for whom, and where — in the first line. A stranger

should know within five seconds whether they're in the right place. No

carousel, no "Welcome to our story," no animated hero.

2. Give them one way to reach you, and make it dead obvious. One. Not phone

and form and chat and newsletter and socials. The most visible one, and the

other three at most in the footer.

3. Prove you're real. One specific, verifiable fact. "Serving [city] since

2009, 400+ jobs." Or one sentence from an actual customer, with a name.

That's the page. Everything else is optional and almost certainly costing you

attention.

The reason most homepages fail

They're written for the owner.

You wrote that homepage to explain your history, your values, your range of

services, and why you care. None of that is what a stranger is checking. A

stranger is checking one thing: **can you do my specific problem, and how do I

start?**

Your history is interesting to you. Your values are important to you. Neither

appears in the decision. The one-line answer to "can you do my problem" and the

phone number are what appear.

The 30-minute version

  • Headline: the problem, named plainly, in their words
  • One paragraph: who you help, where, and what happens after they contact you
  • One proof line
  • One button, or one phone number, large
  • Delete the rest

You will feel that this is too simple and slightly embarrassing. That feeling is

correct and it means you have built a page aimed at customers instead of at

yourself.

Then stop

A page that is clear, current, and answers "can you do my thing" does not need

redesigning every year. **The owners who look professional are almost never the

ones with the newest site** — they're the ones where you understood what they

did within five seconds of landing.

Boring and instantly comprehensible. That's the goal.
